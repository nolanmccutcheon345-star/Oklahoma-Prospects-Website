## baseball — Pitching — 30 minutes

Template: baseball:Pitching:30 · 2026-10-12.1

Objective: Improve one identified development priority and demonstrate transfer.

| Stage | Minutes |
|---|---:|
| Setup | 2 |
| Briefing | 3 |
| General Warm-up | 4 |
| Sport-Specific Warm-up | 4 |
| Drills | 7 |
| Tracking/Assessment | 5 |
| Game IQ | 2 |
| Recap | 3 |

### Instruction sequence

**Setup**

- Confirm the booked athlete, instructor, sport and service. Check attendance and equipment.

**Briefing**

- Review goals, recent assessments, previous feedback and restrictions. Agree on one observable objective.

**General Warm-up**

- Ask about readiness, pain, fatigue and recent training before movement.
- Raise temperature with comfortable locomotion; activate and dynamically mobilize relevant movement patterns.

**Sport-Specific Warm-up**

- Catch play 45 ft: 8 each
- Catch play 60 ft: 8 each
- Long toss stretch: 6 at 75 ft
- Pull down / compact: 6, downhill

**Drills**

- Work one priority with a concise drill and transfer block.
- Rocker Throws: Feet wider than shoulders, side-on to the target. Rock back onto the rear leg, then throw as the weight returns forward.
- Step-Behind Throws: Crossover step behind the front foot, then throw with the momentum created.

**Tracking/Assessment**

- Log representative practice repetitions and compare execution before and after the chosen cue.
- Practice observations stay separate from formally verified recruiting measurements.

**Game IQ**

- Discuss one baseball situation appropriate to this discipline, the athlete’s age and current rules.
- Ask the athlete to explain the decision rather than merely repeat the cue.
- Pre-pitch routine: Build a five-second sequence: get the sign, breathe out, set the feet, pick the target, go. Run it on every pitch of the bullpen, including between pitches. Check: Same routine on 20 of 20 pitches without a reminder.
- Breathing and reset: Teach a 4-second in, 6-second out behind the mound. Trigger it deliberately after any bad pitch in the pen. Check: Athlete resets without being told after a noncompetitive pitch.

**Recap**

- Record what improved, what still needs work, and an achievable next step. Assign existing safe homework with a clear dose and stop conditions.
- Share the family recap; keep instructor observations private. Confirm attendance and documentation before publishing.

### Drills and coaching cues

**Rocker Throws** (d1)

Feet wider than shoulders, side-on to the target. Rock back onto the rear leg, then throw as the weight returns forward.

Dose: 2 sets of 10

Cues: Let the hips go first; Feel the stretch across the front; Finish over a firm front side

Watch for: Chest opening before the hips; Weight stalling on the back leg

**Step-Behind Throws** (d4)

Crossover step behind the front foot, then throw with the momentum created.

Dose: 2 sets of 8

Cues: Build speed into the throw; Stay athletic; Let the ball come out of the movement

Watch for: Rushing the arm ahead of the body

### Safety and workload review

- Stop activity for pain or unusual symptoms; follow existing restrictions and refer concerns to the appropriate professional.
- Review recent games, practices, lessons and the existing workload plan. This template does not grant clearance or prescribe extra throwing.
- Use appropriate equipment, protective screens and a clear training space. Do not chase maximum measurements when fatigued.
- Follow the athlete’s applicable baseball pitch-count/rest restrictions; include lesson throws in workload review.

### Instruction sequence

**Setup**

- Confirm the booked athlete, instructor, sport and service. Check attendance and equipment.

**Briefing**

- Review goals, recent assessments, previous feedback and restrictions. Agree on one observable objective.

**General Warm-up**

- Ask about readiness, pain, fatigue and recent training before movement.
- Raise temperature with comfortable locomotion; activate and dynamically mobilize relevant movement patterns.

**Sport-Specific Warm-up**

- Dry swings: 10, same load every time
- Tee, middle-middle: 8
- Tee, outer third: 8 — only hard oppo counts

**Drills**

- Work one priority with a concise drill and transfer block.
- Balanced tee contact: Use a correctly sized bat, a tee and a clear hitting lane. Rehearse a balanced stance and controlled swings toward a clear target.

**Tracking/Assessment**

- Log representative practice repetitions and compare execution before and after the chosen cue.
- Practice observations stay separate from formally verified recruiting measurements.

**Game IQ**

- Discuss one baseball situation appropriate to this discipline, the athlete’s age and current rules.
- Ask the athlete to explain the decision rather than merely repeat the cue.
- Pre-pitch routine and timing trigger: Same setup, same breath, same load trigger on every rep from the tee through live. Check: Identical routine on 20 swings.
- Owning your zone: Call a zone before each front toss; taking the wrong pitch scores the same as a hit. Check: Swings only in-plan on 8 of 10.

**Recap**

- Record what improved, what still needs work, and an achievable next step. Assign existing safe homework with a clear dose and stop conditions.
- Share the family recap; keep instructor observations private. Confirm attendance and documentation before publishing.

### Drills and coaching cues

**Balanced tee contact** (hit-foundation)

Use a correctly sized bat, a tee and a clear hitting lane. Rehearse a balanced stance and controlled swings toward a clear target.

Dose: Short sets with feedback and rest; prioritize safe, clean contact.

Cues: See the ball; Finish balanced

Watch for: Unsafe bat path near others; Loss of balance

### Safety and workload review

- Stop activity for pain or unusual symptoms; follow existing restrictions and refer concerns to the appropriate professional.
- Review recent games, practices, lessons and the existing workload plan. This template does not grant clearance or prescribe extra throwing.
